Metallic Embroidery Thread Guide: Stop Breakage, Get Perfect Shine

Published June 08, 2025 — EmbroideryConvert Team

Metallic thread is beautiful — and notoriously difficult. Here's how to stitch with it successfully.

Why Metallic Thread Breaks

Metallic thread is a nylon core wrapped in metallic foil. The foil is brittle and frays easily. Standard embroidery settings destroy it.

Settings for Metallic Success

  • Needle: Metallic needle (size 80/12 or 90/14). The larger eye and special coating reduce friction.
  • Speed: Reduce to 400-600 SPM (stitches per minute). Most machines default to 600-800 SPM — too fast for metallic.
  • Tension: Loosen top tension slightly (0.5-1.0). Metallic needs less drag.
  • Thread path: Use a thread stand (vertical spool pin). Horizontal spool feeding twists metallic thread.

Digitizing for Metallic

  • Longer stitches: 3.5-4mm max. Short stitches = more friction on the foil.
  • Simpler designs: Avoid tiny details and dense fills. Metallic works best for outlines and bold elements.

The file format doesn't matter — metallic thread works the same in DST, PES, JEF, etc. Convert to any format and use metallic thread instead of standard.
