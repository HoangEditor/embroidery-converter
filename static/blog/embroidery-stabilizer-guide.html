Embroidery Stabilizer Guide: Which Backing to Use for Every Project

Home › Blog › This Article
Published June 21, 2025 — EmbroideryConvert Team

Stabilizer can make or break your embroidery. Wrong choice = puckered design. Right choice = professional results. Here's the definitive stabilizer guide.

4 Main Types of Stabilizer

1. Cut-Away Stabilizer

Permanent backing that stays on the fabric after trimming. Best for stretchy fabrics (t-shirts, knits, sweatshirts). The stabilizer prevents the design from distorting when the fabric stretches.

2. Tear-Away Stabilizer

Temporary backing — you tear off the excess after stitching. Best for woven fabrics (cotton, denim, canvas) that don't stretch. Easier to remove than cut-away.

3. Wash-Away Stabilizer

Dissolves in water. Best for free-standing lace, sheer fabrics, toppers on towels. Also used as a topper on fluffy fabrics (towels, fleece) to prevent stitches from sinking in.

4. Fusible Stabilizer

Iron-on backing that adheres to fabric. Best for slippery fabrics (silk, satin, nylon) that shift during hooping. Also great for patches and appliqué.

Stabilizer by Fabric Type

  • T-shirts/jerseys: Cut-away + water-soluble topper
  • Denim: Medium tear-away
  • Towels: Tear-away + water-soluble topper
  • Leather/pleather: Stick-on stabilizer (no hoop)
  • Silk/satin: Fusible tear-away
  • Hats: Heavy cut-away
  • Sheer fabric: Wash-away (hoop the stabilizer, float the fabric)

File Conversion Doesn't Affect Stabilizer Choice

Whether your file is DST, PES, or JEF — the stabilizer requirements are the same. The design stitches identically across formats. Convert to any format without worrying about stabilizer changes.
