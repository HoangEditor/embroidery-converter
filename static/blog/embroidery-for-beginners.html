Embroidery for Beginners: From First Stitch to Finished Project (2025)

Published 2025 — EmbroideryConvert Team

Starting embroidery? It's overwhelming. Machines, formats, stabilizers, threads — where do you even begin? This guide walks you through everything in order.

Step 1: Choose Your Machine Type

  • Home embroidery machine: Brother, Janome, Singer, Bernina. Single-needle, 4x4 to 8x12 hoops. $300-$8,000. Best for hobbyists and small business.
  • Commercial machine: Tajima, Barudan, Melco, Ricoma. Multi-needle (6-15 needles), fast, industrial. $8,000-$50,000+. Best for production.

Step 2: Understand File Formats

Your machine only reads its native format. Brother = PES, Janome = JEF, Tajima = DST. Most designs you buy online come in multiple formats. If you get the wrong format, convert it free.

Step 3: Gather Essential Supplies

  • Embroidery thread (polyester or rayon for machine, cotton for hand)
  • Pre-wound bobbins
  • Stabilizer (cut-away for knits, tear-away for wovens)
  • Needles (75/11 embroidery for most projects)
  • Scissors, seam ripper, marking pen

Step 4: Your First Stitch

  1. Download a simple design (fewer than 10,000 stitches, 3 colors max)
  2. Convert to your machine's format if needed
  3. Hoop fabric with stabilizer
  4. Thread machine, insert bobbin
  5. Load design, press start
  6. Watch the first 100 stitches — intervene if thread breaks

Step 5: Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Skipping stabilizer: Design puckers immediately
  • Wrong format: Machine won't read the file
  • Design too dense: Thread breaks constantly
  • Not changing needle: Dull needles ruin fabric
