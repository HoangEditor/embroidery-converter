Embroidery Thread Shelf Life: How Long Does Thread Last & Storage Tips

Home › Blog › This Article
Published June 29, 2025 — EmbroideryConvert Team

That box of thread in your attic from 2015 — is it still good? Here's the truth about thread shelf life and how to store thread so it lasts decades.

Thread Shelf Life by Type

  • Cotton (DMC, Anchor): 20-50+ years if stored properly. Cotton is incredibly stable. Pre-WWII thread still stitches fine if stored in dark, dry conditions.
  • Rayon: 5-15 years. Rayon degrades faster than cotton, especially in humidity. Old rayon may snap during stitching.
  • Polyester: 30-50+ years. Polyester is synthetic and extremely durable. The thread will outlast you.
  • Metallic: 5-10 years. The metallic coating can tarnish or flake over time.

Signs Thread Has Gone Bad

  • Breaks easily: Pull gently — if it snaps with little force, it's degraded
  • Fading: Compare to a fresh skein of the same color
  • Fuzz/dust: Thread shouldn't shed fibers
  • Mold smell: Discard immediately — mold spores spread

Ideal Storage Conditions

  • Dark: UV light fades thread. Store in closed boxes or drawers.
  • Dry: Humidity <50%. Add silica gel packs. Damp thread rots.
  • Cool: Room temperature (60-75°F). Attics and garages get too hot.
  • Dust-free: Covered containers prevent dust accumulation.
